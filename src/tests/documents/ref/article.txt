Abstract

This document exercises the structure of an article: sections, lists, footnotes, references to
labels and theorem-like environments. Its extracted text and its number of pages are com-
pared with stored references, so any change of the layout shows up.

1 Introduction

The typesetter breaks paragraphs into lines with a global algorithm, which takes the whole para-
graph into account when choosing the break points. Long words such as incomprehensibilities,
characteristically and disproportionately invite hyphenation, and a justified paragraph stretches
its spaces so that every line but the last fills the measure. This paragraph is long enough to need
several lines and to show the effect.1

Section 2 shows lists, and Section 3 theorems. Text may be strong, emphasized, verbatim or
in small capitals.

3 Theorems

Definition 1. A prime number is a natural number greater than one which has no divisor other
than one and itself.

Theorem 2. There are infinitely many prime numbers.

Proof. Suppose there are finitely many, multiply them all and add one: the result has a prime
divisor which is none of them.
□

Theorem 2 is due to Euclid.
